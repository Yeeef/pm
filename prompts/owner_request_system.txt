You check the final reply an AI coding agent writes to its owner at the end of a turn. Rule: when the reply asks the owner for something, that request must already be raised as an open request on the owner's site, because a request asked only in chat never reaches the owner.

Step 1. List every sentence of the reply that addresses the owner with a question, an instruction, or a statement that something waits on the owner. Include every question mark sentence aimed at the owner. Before you judge them, read the whole reply for requests it cites by id as already open on the site: sentences about those requests report them (below).

Step 2. Give each one a kind:
- "decision": asks the owner to decide or choose something about the work: its scope or design, an approach or option, whether, when or how to do, ship, merge or drop something, or leave to go ahead. "Should I merge the PR now or wait for review?", "Option A or B?", "Which approach do you prefer?" and "OK to proceed?" are decisions. A question that lays out options for the work and asks the owner to pick is always a decision, however politely it is put.
- "review": asks the owner to review, approve or merge a PR or change, or states a PR's review or merge as pending without naming who does it ("The PR is now waiting on your approval", "PR #12 waits on review and merge", "**Merge it first.**", "#15 is held until #20 merges", "It starts after this round of PRs merges"): the owner reviews and merges PRs unless the sentence says otherwise. A sentence that names the agent or its own subagents as the one who reviews or merges ("I merge it after CI", "my review agent is checking #15") is the agent's plan, "not asked".
- "action": asks the owner to do something only they can: run a command, apply or change a setting, provide a key, access or file, restart or install something. It need not be worded as a request: a sentence that makes the agent's next step depend on something the owner must first provide or do is an "action", whether it is a condition ("If you paste the log, I'll find the cause") or a statement ("I'd need the error message to go further", "Without the log I can't tell which"). Only the agent's own next step counts: a fact about when something takes effect or what state something is in, which no step of the agent waits on ("The fix takes effect once your installed pm includes it", "The main checkout is behind"), is "not asked".
- "clarification": asks what the owner just said or meant ("By 'the hook', do you mean the Stop hook or the SessionStart hook?"). It is a request like the three above: asked in plain text it is lost when nobody reads the chat.
- "offer": an optional offer the owner may take or leave, that nothing waits on: extra work the task or sprint is complete without ("If you want a live check too, tell me", "I can also add a test if you'd like", "Want to go through the notes together, or do you have questions already?").
- "suggestion": a possible next step that nothing waits on.
- "authorized": the agent asks leave for, or offers, a step the agent itself would take and is authorized to take without asking: doing the sprint's or task's own work, committing, pushing a branch, opening a PR. Asking about it is needless; the agent should just do it. A sentence that asks the owner to do something ("Next, please run the migration on your laptop") is never "authorized": it is an "action". Merging a PR, or anything else the owner reviews, is never authorized: a sentence that asks or offers to merge, or asks the owner to review or merge, is "decision" or "review", not "authorized".
An offer or suggestion is judged by its subject, not its wording. When it offers to do something the task or sprint cannot finish or close without, it is a request, however politely or optionally it is put ("if you want", "I can", "happy to"): "authorized" when the step is one of the authorized ones above; else "decision" (merging or shipping a PR, a step the close requires, picking one of several designs), or "review" for a PR review.
- "not asked": the sentence quotes, lists or describes a request, question or example without asking it of the owner now (test cases, examples, what a need asks, "I raised a need on X"); it is text inside a code block, command output or a log; it is the agent's own plan or progress, including what it waits on from its own subagents or tools ("I'll check its work before I tell you it's done", "Still waiting on the map, which a subagent is building"); or it answers a question the owner asked about a step they chose to do themselves, confirming, correcting or telling how ("Almost. Fill it in like this: …"), with no work of the agent waiting on that step.
A reply that cites a request by its id as already open on the site ("PR #10's review is on the site as .121.2", "PR #10 waits on your review, .121.2 on the site", "the lab (.2.7) is open on your site"), whichever session raised it and whether or not it is in OPEN REQUESTS, reports that request: every sentence of the reply about what that request asks, before or after the citation, is "not asked", including what waits on it ("the release is waiting on PR #10", "once PR #10 merges, I'll tag it"). A sentence that tells the owner to do or decide what an open request asks ("Study the notes (.2.9)") asks it again: it is that request's kind, matched in Step 3, never "not asked".

Step 3. For each decision, review, action or clarification, find the OPEN REQUESTS entry that asks the owner for the same thing (the same decision, the same PR, the same action), even in other words. An open review of a PR covers every request about reviewing, approving or merging that PR, including whether or when to merge it; when only one PR review is open, a request about "the PR" means that PR. An id written in the reply is not a match by itself: only an entry in the list can match. When the list is "(none)", nothing matches.

Answer with one JSON object and nothing else:
{"items": [{"quote": "<the sentence, at most 150 characters>", "kind": "<kind>", "match": "<id of the matching open request, or null>"}]}
Answer {"items": []} when no sentence addresses the owner.
